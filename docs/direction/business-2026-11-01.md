1. **The first real user is blocked by the owner and the vendor, not by code.** The launch gate is merged and fails
   closed (VERIFIED, `lib/launch-gate.ts`). What stands between today and the first allowlisted connect:

   and the vendor's terms bar disclosing end-user data to third parties without written consent. So every P6 item and
   the vendor's written reply on closed-trade percentages come **before invitee 1 connects**, not before
   `LAUNCH_OPEN=1` (VERIFIED, POLICY.md P6; Appendix D, legal-market).
3. **The vendor letter in pass 1 was wrong in five ways.** Sent as drafted, it would have become a misleading
   evidence file:
   - it said we store no quantities or prices, but the raw `activities` table does;
   - it stated a once-a-day cadence, but the code syncs each trader up to hourly with a 7-day re-read;
   - it described freeze wrongly;
   - it understated the open-position surfaces of F-6;
   - it understated the weighted headline and cash-flow data F-5 needs.

   The corrected letter is Appendix A (VERIFIED against `db/schema.sql`, `lib/sync.ts`, F-5, F-6).
4. **Three launch-relevant gaps nobody had listed** (VERIFIED, Appendix D, repo and vendor-costs):
   - the hourly sync can't run on Vercel as built, because Vercel Cron sends GET and `/api/sync` exports only POST,
     and `vercel.json` has no cron entry;
   - F-6 unrealized percentages are public for every trader, with no market-data gate in code;
   - nothing deletes the vendor connection when a record freezes, so every frozen record keeps costing about $1.03 a
     month.
5. **Trust and safety rules U2 and U3 are unbuilt** (VERIFIED, Appendix D, repo):
   - no report action;
   - no reserved-handle list, and a rename frees the old handle at once;
   - no takedown text;
   - no accessibility tooling (no automated accessibility checker).

   All are small agent slices. They need the owner only for wording and for one dev dependency.
6. **F-7 bypasses the launch gate.** People named in public filings never sign up or connect, so the gate and P6 never
   see them. Their profiles would go public with the first production deploy. The legislator source has a statutory
   bar on commercial use (5 U.S.C. § 13107(c), VERIFIED as cited by red team A; news-media exception), and F-7 sits
   in tension with POLICY T2. The council recommends a separate real-filer switch, off by default. It also recommends
   re-scoping F-7 to company-insider filings, and leaving the legal calls to the owner (section 6).
7. **Unit economics are small and benign, but beside the point.**
   - Fixed cost is about $145/month.
   - Each connected trader costs $1.03/month.
   - Break-even is 23 paying viewers at 50 traders.
   - Revenue is $0 through week 8, because billing stays off.

   **The one number to remember: one hour of the build loop (~$290) costs about two months of running the beta.**
   (ESTIMATE; section 4.)
8. **The week-8 clock starts at the first real connect, 3-6 weeks out.** Fifty traders by week 8 is the high case: at
   the market council's 4% one-to-one funnel, that is about 1,250 contacts and about 46 owner hours. Plan for week 12,

**Revenue (viewers only: F-3 has traders never pay, and POLICY F5 bars selling subscriptions to traders; VERIFIED):**
- At the market council's ceiling ($9.99/month, $99.99/year), Stripe takes 2.9% + $0.30, Billing 0.7%, Tax 0.5%

| Traders | Payers to break even | Monthly viewers needed at 0.25% / 0.5% / 1.5% conversion |
|---|---|---|
| fixed cost only | 17 | 6,800 / 3,400 / 1,133 |
| 50 | 23 | 9,200 / 4,600 / 1,533 |
| 1,000 | 134 | 53,600 / 26,800 / 8,933 |

ESTIMATE, recomputed in Appendix D (vendor-costs). The market council's prior for this kind of product is 0.1-0.5%,
so the 0.25% column is the honest headline. 1.5% is the optimistic edge. Above about 8.5 connected traders per payer,
each new trader loses money.

**What this means (OPINION):**
- Infrastructure is not the money question. Through week 8, revenue is $0, because `LIVE_BILLING` stays off. So the
  gates can't test willingness to pay; a priced "notify me" control (idea 3) is the only intent signal before billing.
- A 50-trader beta would need about 184 monthly uniques per trader at 0.25% (9,200 / 50) to break even. Growth's central guess is
  about 40. That is fine for a learning beta, and fatal as a business at that ratio. The stop line belongs in the
  pre-registered gate reading (proposal P7).

**Cheapest path to the week-8 gates (ESTIMATE):**
1. Keep billing off.
2. Stay on the Daily plan.
3. Delete the vendor connection when a record freezes, if the vendor confirms that derived percentages may stay
   public (letter Q2). Otherwise each frozen record costs about $12 a year.
4. Recruit by hand. At 4% connect per one-to-one contact (the market council's funnel), 50 traders is about 1,250
   contacts and about 46 owner hours.
5. Plan for week 12. Read week 8 as an interim result.
6. Ship first-party measurement before wave 1: a `?via=` source class, an aggregate view counter, share events and a
   D7 first-seen marker. Without it, three of the four gates can't be read at week 8.

**Levers, ranked by merged work per dollar (OPINION):**
1. **Merge before start.** No new feature PR while 5 are open. Rebase the oldest first.
2. **Per-PR cost log.** Each session records the PR it served. Report dollars per merged feature weekly.
3. **Repo fact-check before drafting** in every council and plan: one cheap agent produces a "state of main" note.
4. **Cheaper model tier for drafting and debate passes.** The default tier is kept for fact-checks, red teams, code and

5. **Councils only for new questions.** Keep a "deferred until Open" table in `docs/DECISIONS.md` so agents stop
   re-raising settled items.
6. **Cap design-round mandates.** For F-8: 5 rounds with a 7-member panel (about $900), stop early when two rounds
   change nothing, and go further only with the owner's written go-ahead.
7. **Skip e2e on docs-only PRs**, if CI doesn't already (protected path; owner).

### 6. New since the brief: F-7 and F-8

**F-7 (public traders).** The owner's direction stands. These recommendations re-scope it. They don't overrule it.
1. **A separate production switch for real-filer data**, off by default, with a CI test. Real filers stay hidden
   until the owner signs off each source. The launch gate can't cover them, because filers never sign up (red team A,
   HIGH).
2. **Legislator reports: no fetching code until the owner rules on it.** 5 U.S.C. § 13107(c) makes it unlawful to
   obtain or use these reports for a commercial purpose, with an exception for news and communications media
   disseminating to the general public. Whether a record publisher fits that exception is a legal call. POLICY leaves
   risk calls the committee findings leave open to the owner, so F-7's "committee decides" can't settle it.
3. **Start with company-insider filings.** They carry dates and prices on both sides, so a per-trade percent can be
   computed without guessing. Drop quarterly holdings reports: with no trade dates, any per-trade return would be
   guessed, which breaks the "never guessed" rule.
4. **No Verified badge, and a separate labelled list.** Filers stay out of the verified ranking. Calling the profiles
   "identical" to verified ones invites a misleading-comparison claim.
5. **Make one market-data decision for F-6 and F-7.** Both may need prices from outside the broker. A new vendor or
   dependency is the owner's call (POLICY H).
6. **Clarify T2 in writing.** T2 says never name real people "in personas, copy, demo data or docs". F-7 puts real
   filers' names in production data. A one-line owner clarification (production data from public filings, never in
   the repository) removes the conflict. Agents can't make that change.
7. Risks the per-source review must cover:
   - defamation from a wrong return;
   - right of publicity for insiders who aren't public figures;
   - a corrections and removal path that is live before any real filer is shown.

**F-8 (prediction game).** It is design only and has no launch effect. Its one risk is spend (section 5). The council
also notes POLICY F6's bar on wagering, prizes and rewards for trading activity. A game that rewards predictions
needs the approval council's check against F6 before it is marked ready.

##### Legal findings in brief
- **FTC Safeguards Rule (GLBA).** Coverage is plausible but not certain. 16 CFR 314.2(h)(1) defines a "financial
  institution" as any institution significantly engaged in activities that are financial in nature under BHCA §4(k).
  Those activities include data processing of financial data, and the examples list includes a "finder". 314.1(b)
  also reaches customer information that other financial institutions provide to you. A read-only publisher of
  broker-derived records sits close enough that COMPLIANCE §3/§10 are right to comply.
  The new point is cost. **16 CFR 314.6** exempts institutions holding customer information on fewer than 5,000
  consumers from four requirements: 314.4(b)(1) written risk assessment, (d)(2) continuous monitoring or annual
  penetration testing, (h) written incident response plan and (i) annual board report. Everything else still applies:
  a qualified individual, access controls, encryption, MFA, training, vendor oversight, disposal, and FTC notice
  within 30 days of a breach affecting 500+ consumers (314.4(j)). L13 stays, but it is lighter during the beta. Put
  the 30-day FTC notice in the incident runbook.
- **State minors' laws.** Several states regulate accounts and feeds for under-18s, with varying age-assurance duties.
  Examples:
  - California SB 976 (Health & Safety Code §27000 et seq.) bars "addictive feeds" for minors. From 2027-01-01 it
    covers users the operator has *reasonably determined* are minors, not only known minors, under AG age-assurance
    rules. A rulemaking notice is published; adoption is not confirmed.
  - Texas HB 18 (Bus. & Com. Code ch. 509) requires age registration at account creation for digital services that
    let users post and interact.
  - Florida HB 3 (Fla. Stat. §501.1736) restricts accounts for under-14s and under-16s on platforms with addictive
    features that meet usage thresholds.

  Parts of several are enjoined or in litigation; check status before relying on them. The current feed ranks by
  `abs(return_pct)` for everyone, so it is not personalised. F-2 PR-5/6 personalisation is what most plausibly makes
  it an "addictive feed" under SB 976. BL's 2027-01-01 date is VERIFIED. 18+ attestation is a mitigation, not an
  exemption (COMPLIANCE.md:120).
- **DMCA §512.**
  - Safe harbor needs a designated agent filed with the Copyright Office (§512(c)(2)) and a repeat-infringer policy
    that is adopted, reasonably implemented and told to users (§512(i)(1)(A)).
  - Fee: **$6** per designation. The designation **expires after 3 years** unless renewed by amendment or resubmission.
  - The directory is public. The agent's address may be a PO box. The *service provider's* address may be a PO box
    only with the Office's prior approval in exceptional cases. A sole proprietor would therefore publish a street
    address, which supports forming the entity first.
  - Registration is legally optional: without it there is simply no safe harbor (trust & safety is right on that).
    POLICY U3 requires a takedown process, and COMPLIANCE.md:111 makes registration a rule before any user text goes
    live. At $6 and about 20 minutes, there is little reason to defer it.
- **Publisher's exclusion conditions (COMPLIANCE §2, lines 56-66).** Legal's L12 list matches: no "you should", no
  personalised picks, no "top traders to follow", no paid signals, a documented ranking that treats gains and losses
  equally, a disclaimer on every performance surface (procedures, line 64), no paid visibility (anti-touting row).
  It leaves out the statutory element itself: a *bona fide* publication of *general and regular circulation*
  (15 U.S.C. 80b-2(a)(11)(D)). Add it, because F-2 personalisation and F-3 paid tools press on "general".
- **F3b and invite emails (POLICY.md:120-126).** The carve-out allows only messages with no handle, symbol, return or
  record field, sent on account or billing events. A platform-sent second-degree invite that names the inviting
  trader is outside the carve-out. A platform invite with no record content is not barred by F3b's text, but it is
  still commercial email under CAN-SPAM. Any founder message citing a trader's record is inside the bar ("any
  channel"). Legal's fix, where the trader shares the link through the system share sheet and the platform sends
  nothing, is right.
