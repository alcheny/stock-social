# Token budget governor: review log, 2026-11-01

Process (owner: "extensively review and test code before using this feature, with multiple rounds"):
spec (docs/token-budget.md) → engineer subagent writes tests first, then code → adversarial tester with
mutation testing → independent review rounds by fresh reviewers (correctness and edge cases; estimation
maths and calibration; CLI usability) → fix every verified finding → another round until a round finds
nothing blocking. Every reviewer had no stake in the code and saw the code, not the author's conclusions.

## Owner corrections during the build (relayed by the orchestrator)

1. Usage must not stop before 100% of the cap: `allowed_warning` is calibration only, never a brake;
   admit while spend + running reserve + new estimates ≤ 100% of the cap; only rejected/overage brake.
2. "Do not limit rate": no per-hour limits, pacing or concurrency caps.
3. "If unsure about if new work will go over limit cap, go with the more conservative estimate and risk
   going over the cap": admit when uncertain (new items at their low estimate, running at point, cap at
   its high end); ranges low/point/high in the output; overshoot risk reported.
4. "Ideally, at the end, I should have no tokens left": `fill` recommendation for spare budget.
5. Every known limit carries a `// ponytail:` comment and is listed in the PR.

Engineer: 45 tests written first (seen failing), then the planner; 1,500-case seeded property test.

## Round 1

Three reviewers in parallel, plus the tester (separate section).

**Correctness and edge cases** — blocking: yes.
- B1 (blocking) `iso()` throws `RangeError` on prototype-key window types (`constructor`), times beyond the
  Date range, and a tiny burn making `capHitAt` overflow — breaks "never throw".
- B2 (blocking) a `rejected` reading from an old window caps every later window, overriding a newer warning.
- B3 (blocking) a tick without `resetsAt` writes a `historyEntry` stamped with an invented window that
  wins calibration on the next tick (cap overestimated: unsafe).
- M1 cap carried over from an older window is not raised by the current window's `allowed` evidence.
- M2 duplicate-session tie keeps the not-done copy. M3 generated ids (`#1`) can collide with real ids.
- M4 float boundary: item with low $0.20 queued against $0.20 left.
- Nits: missing window type silently defaults; orchestrator tail in the spec table unused; `recent` above
  cost falls back silently; property generator never produces the B1 inputs.

**Estimation maths and calibration** — blocking: yes. All fixture numbers re-derived by hand and matched.
- 1 (blocking) same as M1: a carried-over calibration throttles every later window although the current
  window's readings prove the cap is higher.
- 2 lifetime burn of very young sessions (minutes old) blows past the prior's high end (a $3.16 council at
  5 minutes reserves $49.51), over-reserving when many start together.
- 3 the fixture's history is inconsistent with its sessions (history slope $296/h vs sessions' $74/h); the
  plan never flags such a mismatch and contradicts itself (cap hit at 12:00 vs $364 unused at reset).
- 4 council high prior 50 is already disproven by a council at $48.31 still fixing.
- 5 calibrated point = high; point should sit between A/warn and W/warn.
- Nits: safety widens an already-high figure; admitted at low, reserved at point next tick (by owner rule);
  remaining not cut at reset. No hidden rate or concurrency limit found.

**CLI usability** — blocking: yes.
- 1 (blocking) `rateLimitType` (the provider's field name) is ignored: a five_hour window passed straight
  through is treated as seven_day with no issue.
- 2 (blocking) projection (cap hit 12:00 at the history burn) contradicts the decision ("queue more work")
  without explanation.
- 3 (blocking) same as correctness B2, and `calibratedFrom` names the wrong reading.
- Minor: pause list is every session with no count; `stallRisk` true with nothing to do; `fill.message`
  under overage says everything is admitted; invented reset time shown as fact when the window is missing;
  exit-code rule underspecified; `lowerBoundUsd` above cap high after a rejected reading; history upkeep
  not documented; no rule for mixed windows across children; doc says `admit` lists ids (it lists objects).
- Nits: milliseconds in ISO times; `Usd` suffix inconsistent; `fill.maxItemHours` duplicates
  `window.hoursToReset`; doc doesn't map child-session fields to snapshot fields.

**Tester (adversarial + mutation)** — 7 bugs, 5 of them crashes; 15 tests added (45 → 60, all pass).
- Crashes: inherited-key window type; timestamps outside the Date range (ns epochs); tiny burn overflowing
  `capHitAt`; ~130k history readings overflowing the stack (`Math.max(...array)`); not a crash but silent:
  1e308 values producing Infinity (JSON null) with no issue.
- Logic: `resetsAt` more than one window ahead puts the window start after `now`, spend reads $0 and a $100
  cap is overrun; bad-status warning text emitted before the rollover check.
- Held up: null/array/scalar shapes, string numbers, prototype-key ids/kinds/priors, rollovers, out-of-order
  and future history, negative/NaN/inverted estimates, 10k sessions + queue + history in ~37 ms, every CLI
  malformed-input path (exit 2 with JSON).
- Mutation testing (scratch script, 69 mutants of every rule): 57/69 killed by the original tests; the 15
  added tests raised it to 68/69. The survivor (not resetting `lastActiveAt` to `startedAt` when it is
  earlier) is equivalent: both readers of that field give identical output.

**Round 1 fixes** were handed to the engineer as one list (20 items: every blocking and minor finding above
plus the doc nits).

Fixes (engineer, tests first; the tester's 7 bug repros were ported and seen failing): all 20 items done.

## Round 2

Three new reviewers. Clean on 20,000 fuzzed hostile snapshots (no throws, no non-finite output, deterministic)
and all fixture numbers re-derived by hand; still blocking findings in every lens.

**Correctness** — blocking: yes.
- 1 (blocking) a `historyEntry` written without a usable window counts pre-window sessions in full; on the next
  tick it is placed in the current window and inflates spend ($910 instead of $10) and raises the cap.
- 2 a rejection in a newer window than the warning does not replace the stale warning (later windows ~13%
  under-admitted; clamp message names the wrong reading).
- 3 history has no window type: a seven_day reading whose `resetsAt` lines up on the five_hour grid lands in the
  five_hour window.
- Nits: no epsilon in projection comparisons; `warning: true` reported for an unusable window.

**Estimation maths** — blocking: yes. All numbers matched by hand.
- F1 (blocking) a carried-over cap that this window disproves is raised to the lower bound and used as HIGH:
  headroom = spend/3 − reserve, nothing admitted for the rest of the window (worse than no calibration).
- F2 a cap high below observed non-rejected spend is never raised within the window.
- F3 the orchestrator's ~$296/h is not consistent with the sessions (they sum to ~$74/h; the figure equals the
  slope from a history reading ~$234 below what the sessions account for). `burn-mismatch` changes nothing:
  real outside spend is reserved nowhere, an undercounting history is trusted.
- F4 (owner decision) admitting at LOW can admit a deep queue the planner itself labels `likely` over.
- F5 near reset, post-reset work is charged in full to this window, so fill under-fills.
- F6–F9 nits: 30-minute cliff (bounded, ≤ ~$115 for ≤ 25 min); admitted highs lack safety; strict-stop slack
  ≤ one item; orchestrator lifetime rate understates. No hidden rate or concurrency limit.

**CLI usability** (snapshots built from the doc alone) — blocking: yes.
- B1 (blocking) mixed windows: one shared history lets a five_hour warning calibrate the seven_day cap ($58.67).
- B2 (blocking) the pause rule ("until status is allowed") cannot be followed under overage (status is already
  allowed), and resuming is undocumented.
- Minor: `nextAdmitAt` = reset (even 17 days out) under an unknown-status brake; brake name for window-ahead;
  invented `allowed` after rollover; non-boolean `isUsingOverage` fails open; bad costs only warn; fill suggests
  `build:small` that the queue cannot express; output schema undocumented; cap-hit time without a date;
  `stallRisk` double meaning; fill half null when cap unknown. Nits: naming, `reasonCode`, shortfall text.

Fixes handed to the engineer as one list of 20 items. F4 kept at the owner's explicit rule (admit at LOW), with
a `config.admitAt` switch and the overshoot tier documenting the consequence.

**Orchestrator correction during round 2 fixes.** The orchestrator supplied primary readings (session
creation times and costs): sessions were ~1-1.5 h old, not the 3-5 h the fixture had invented, so the ~$294/h
aggregate is real (~$22/h per live session) and the reviewer's "$74/h" came from the fixture. Fixture rebuilt
from the real times; priors and typical hours recalibrated (builds 1.5 h, councils 1.25 h); young-session
threshold lowered to 10 minutes; the unattributed-burn reserve dropped (no account-level total exists), so
`burn-mismatch` is informational.

Fixes (tests first, 45 seen failing): all 20 items plus corrections A-D. 104 tests pass; the round-2 fuzz
(20,000 cases) has 0 throws, 0 non-finite, 0 nondeterminism. Mutation: 136 mutants (every earlier mutant
rewritten for the current code plus new ones for every round-2 rule) run in isolated copies by a parallel
runner: 133 killed, 3 survivors each killed by a new test, final 136/136.

## Round 4

Three new reviewers with an explicit bar: blocking = a wrong start/hold/pause/resume decision on plausible input,
a wrong cap, a throw, nondeterminism, or the doc contradicting the code on a decision.

**Correctness** — blocking: yes. A new differential checker (60,000 snapshots over 3 seeds, recomputing the
admission sums, the first misfit, fill-implies-admission, pause/resume gating and determinism from the output)
found no violations; two decision bugs found by hand:
- 1 (blocking) an item that carries nothing past the reset is still refused by the next-window test once running
  carry-over exceeds the cap, while fill recommends that very kind.
- 2 (blocking) once non-rejected spend passes a calibrated high, the cap is set to current spend every tick:
  nothing admitted or resumed until a rejection or the reset (days, on seven_day).
- Minor: overrun sessions never carry over; pause rule under rejected + overage; exact equality on window ids.

**Estimation maths** — blocking: no. Every number re-derived by hand across 7 scenarios (incl. 0.3 h before
reset with 40 and 200 builds, overrun sessions, just after reset with the orchestrator lookahead). Minor:
overrun sessions and the orchestrator never carry over; projection jump at the 10-minute mark; head-of-line
blocking near reset by an over-next-window item; rounding/wording nits; priors on the cautious side.

**CLI usability** (a 21-tick simulated day from the doc alone, with history, warning, near-reset fill, a
rejected episode, rollover, mixed windows, garbage) — blocking: yes.
- B1 (blocking) resume can deadlock: paused reserves keep `runningFits` false forever, across rollovers too;
  and after rejected → allowed in the same window the cap tracks spend ("cap reached" while allowed).
- B2 (blocking) the summary drops "queue more work" (straight-line cap hit) while `fill` asks for more work.
- Minor: `recent` with short ticks; error issues with brake none; mixed-window table gaps; grid-inferred
  history after rollover; resume/admit wording; held sessions not in the summary; carried cap ignores the old
  window's later allowed readings (safe side).

Fixes handed to the engineer as one list of 13 items.

Fixes (tests first, 14 seen failing): all 13 items. One was reverted after measurement: a reserve-implied

## Round 5

Three new reviewers, same bar. The maths reviewer re-derived ~10 scenarios to the cent (incl. cap-disproved,
rejection-cleared, resume gating with 3 paused sessions, overrun/orchestrator carry 0.5 h before reset, 40 builds
0.3 h before reset); the correctness reviewer's calibration fuzzer (12,000 cases, 4 seeds: determinism, history
order, cap low ≤ high, monotonicity in the cap, resume/fill consistency) found nothing beyond bug 1; the usability
reviewer ran a literal orchestrator for 30 ticks against three provider truths. Remaining blocking findings:
- (maths + correctness) a calibration disproved in its own window comes back after the reset (the seen check only
  looked at the current window); the fleet is throttled until spend passes it again.
- (correctness + usability) time spent paused counts as age, so a resumed session looks like a long overrun and
  reserves several times what it has left ($121.88 for ~$2.50); a paused session with no `recent` reading grows
  its reserve until the queue blocks and it never resumes.
- (correctness) a `resetsAt` 1 s earlier classes the previous window as overlapping and drops its calibration.
- (usability) two window plans can name different `resume.next` ids with no way to pick a common one.
Minor: unknown-cap wording (allowed vs allowed_warning); mixed-window fill with null and brake precedence; `done`
vs paused; cold start with no reading; wording of `bad-cost`; burn-mismatch noise; per-item carry in fill;
the doc's $295/h vs $294/h claim is partly coincidence ($257/h fixture aggregate); same-`at` tie order.

Fixes handed to the engineer as one list of 14 items.

Fixes (tests first): all 14 items. 147 tests pass. Mutation: 210 mutants, 204 killed; the 6 survivors were
equivalent, 5 of them in a current-window block made unreachable by the new source-window check (a probe over
~600,000 random cap estimates never fired it), so that block was deleted. Fuzzers (calibration fuzzer on 7

## Round 6

Three new reviewers. The maths reviewer re-derived the fixture and six scenarios by hand (incl. disproof after
the reset, rejection rescue, ±30 s jitter over 24 ticks, bootstrap, 40 builds 0.3 h before reset); the
correctness reviewer's new fuzzer (jitter invariance, same-time vs +1 s rejection splits) was clean; the
usability reviewer ran a literal orchestrator against a hidden true cap ($300, five_hour, off-grid next
window, jittered `resetsAt`) for 145 ticks with no inconsistency between summary and fields. Blocking:
- (all three) `pausedHours` "so far" is subtracted twice while a session is paused or just resumed
  (reserve $210 instead of $22.50; a resumable session is held).
- (maths) a disproved calibration comes back two windows later (only its own and the current window checked).
- (usability, also found as minor by the others) bootstrap never fires: the orchestrator lists itself as
  running, and the cold-start tick's own `historyEntry` disables it.
- (usability) pro-rata window spend over the whole span undercounts sessions paused across a reset; the
  simulated second window calibrated $218-238 against a true $300.
Minor: same-instant rejection ordering; bootstrap text; `resume.fitting` undocumented in the schema; quiet
period stuck by unparsed times; lower bound under disproval; summary silent on next window over cap; noise
from cold-start history; stall time as active age; slow one-per-tick recovery; implausible `resetsAt`.

Fixes handed to the engineer as one list of 13 items.

Fixes (tests first): all 13 items, plus one design change found in the simulation and accepted: window spend
is computed twice, with paused hours placed before the window start for admission (higher spend, safe for
admitting) and inside the window for cap evidence and `historyEntry` (lower spend, safe for calibration);
they differ only for a session spanning the window start with paused hours and no `windowStartCostUsd`.
171 tests pass. Mutation: 256 mutants, 252 killed, 4 equivalent. Fuzzers clean on 5 seeds; the literal
orchestrator keeps the cap through 40 ticks in both modes.

## Round 7

Three new reviewers. One narrow blocking item each; everything else re-derived by hand or fuzzed clean (new
fuzzer: reorder invariance, monotonicity in the cap and in cost, resume.all consistency; 4 seeds x 1,500).
The usability reviewer's own literal orchestrator (30+ runs, 360-1,600 ticks, hidden true cap, jittered and
off-grid windows, invisible background spend, overage mode, mixed windows) measured utilisation at reset of
0.99-1.06 of the true cap (median ~1.002) for five_hour and 1.00 for seven_day, with no contradictions and no
CLI/`plan()` mismatch; overshoot stays within the documented admit-at-low risk.
- (correctness) a pause that ended counts as active once the session goes idle (wrong admit via cap evidence
  and via the reserve).
- (maths) a `recent` reading from before a pause dilutes the burn over the pause (reserve $33.75 → $4.82).
- (usability) an off-grid current window plus jittered `resetsAt` splits an older window into many, so a real
  rejection is ignored and the cap comes out ~9% above it.
Minor: `cap-stale` drops the cap on 1% excess; later windows can drop but not raise low; history slope mixes
spend figures; `windowStartCostUsd` checked before "finished before the window"; doc gaps (pruning by 60 s,
per-window `windowStartCostUsd`, retention, combined resume.all, bootstrap with paused children, invisible
spend wording); duplicate-id tie order.

Fixes handed to the engineer as one list of 9 items.

Fixes (tests first): all 9 items (one deviation accepted: a not-paused session whose `pausedHours` exceeds its
span uses the paused formula, since such a pause cannot lie inside the span). 186 tests pass. Mutation: 281
mutants, 277 killed, 4 equivalent. Fuzzers clean. Simulation, 9 runs, mean utilisation 0.995 → 0.990 and
rejected share 0.191 → 0.205; the off-grid drop traced mostly to the simulation not following the doc (pruning
"previous window" by grid, late `windowStartCostUsd`), now spelled out in the doc; a doc-conformant copy gives
0.986 / 0.192. The round-6 split of spend figures stays (dropping it was inconclusive).

## Round 8

Three new reviewers.
- **Estimation maths — blocking: no.** Every number re-derived (fixture, three pause cases with and without
  `recent.pausedHours`, evidence 5%/15% above a calibrated high, jittered history, 40 builds 0.3 h before

  non-monotonicity near the cap. New fuzzer (jitter, off-grid older windows, pauses) otherwise clean.
- **CLI usability — blocking: yes (one item).** Doc-literal simulator (5 scenarios x 4 seeds, 80-130 ticks,

  60 s splits a window. Rejected-tick share 0-48% with clean input, by the owner's admit-at-low design.
