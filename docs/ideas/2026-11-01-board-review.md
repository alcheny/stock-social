## How to read this record

- **O** marks an idea the owner already decided. The board never overrules it. It flags risks and proposes
  conditions. Status is **adopted with conditions** (the board's conditions go to the owner as proposals) or
  **flagged for owner** (a conflict with an existing decision, or a flag strong enough to need the owner's call).
- **D** marks an idea the owner delegated (owner's instruction of 2026-11-01). The chair's recommended option
  becomes the decision. Status is **adopted**, **adopted with conditions**, **testing**, **rejected** or
  **flagged for owner**.
- Every section starts with the decisions keeper's conflicts and ends with the common-sense check
  (`team/idea-board.md`). An idea that contradicts an existing decision is never adopted as stated: the board
  proposes a version that fits, or flags it.
- Verdict tables show each member's round-1 verdict, then round-2 verdict. "Flag" means flag to the owner.
- Facts are marked **verified**, **partly true** (corrected version given) or **UNVERIFIED**.
- Idea 12 is special. POLICY H reserves the final Terms and Privacy Policy text to the owner. The board fixes
  the option the private drafts use. The owner approves the text. The owner fills the entity name, addresses and
  contacts; the board never invents them.
- Ideas that add new functions or requirements are marked **candidate** in the register in `docs/IDEAS.md`. They
  go to the approval council next (`team/approval-council.md`), which votes on which become feature files. The
  candidates from this review: idea 13 (the week-8 gate counters and the report action it requires before week
  0), idea 14 (the honest priced notify-me test), and D20-D27 and D29. Owner-decided items and pure Terms and
  Privacy options are not candidates.

## Cross-cutting findings

These came up across ideas. Each is a fact in the repo or the law, checked in round 3. Most need an owner edit,
because agents can't change POLICY, DESIGN, COMPLIANCE or the feature files' decisions.

1. **F-2 deletes what F-2 keeps.** F-2's acceptance criterion "Raw events older than 90 days are deleted by a
   nightly job" contradicts F-2's 2026-11-01 decision ("stored indefinitely … nothing is deleted on a timer").
   F-2 is `ready`, so the pipeline could build either. `lib/discovery/purge.ts` already deletes ended "Not
   interested" rows after one day and says PR-5 extends that to the event log. Verified (repo).
2. **The range has three definitions.** DESIGN TR-9: log returns, cluster-robust, open positions included.
   F-4 (2026-10-31): a 95% range around the arithmetic average of closed trades. F-6: the range reflects open
   positions, shown realized and unrealized apart. The built code is closed-trades-only (`lib/trades.ts`, the
   `ponytail:` comment near line 208). TR-9's span also runs "to today" with open positions, while F-4 runs to
   the last close. Verified (repo). One owner rule must govern before the F-4 rework and the F-6 build collide.
3. **The Doctor merges protected loop files with no human, and there is no global spend cap.** The Gate merges
   Doctor PRs once CI and review are green, skipping the CODEOWNERS protected-path check
   (`scripts/autopilot.ts`, the `g.doctor` branch). That reaches the Gate's own logic, `scripts/team.ts` (budget
   scaling, the protected list), `scripts/plan.ts`, `team/`, `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/AUTOPILOT.md`. So "protected
   paths are never auto-merged" is not literally true. `scripts/invariants.test.ts` pins merge rules, not spend.
   `DAILY_PR_CAP` defaults to 0 (no cap). Worst case for one stuck PR is about $7,400 in team-call ceilings
   (scales 1 to 4 over 15 attempts at $150), times `FEATURE_PARALLEL` (default 3). These are ceilings, not
   actual spend. Verified (repo).
4. **Two of the four week-8 gates can't be measured today.** Nothing counts external-referrer profile views
   (gate 3) or viewer D7 (gate 4). Share columns and referral attribution exist (gates 1 and 2). A Referer-only
   counter would undercount, because in-app browsers often strip it, so a share marker on copied links is
   needed. Verified (repo); the Referer behavior is partly true (practitioner reports, no official documentation).
5. **The data vendor's developer terms restrict redistribution and re-packaging.** SnapTrade's Developer Terms
   (updated 2026-05-22): market data may "only be used to serve the End User" it was licensed for; clients may
   "not store, resell, or redistribute Market Data"; and may not "mine, re-sell, or re-package any End User data
   … to third parties" without "the express written consent of both the End User and SnapTrade". Its
   Application Compliance Policy requires compliance before production and lets the vendor cut access. Meaning:
   unrealized values on public pages (F-6) conflict unless the vendor agrees in writing; and the public record
   itself may need the vendor's written confirmation, not only F-5 sizing. Commercial use needs a paid plan. No
   listed plan covers public display. Verified (vendor pages).
6. **Feed ordering by likes, and the California and New York minors' feed laws from 2027.** Cal. Health & Safety
   Code §27000.5 treats a feed ordered on "previous interactions with media" as an addictive feed. Its exemption
   covers only what the user "expressly and unambiguously requested" (follows, blocks), and only if nothing else
   orders it. F-2's like and reject fading is ordering on prior interactions, so it is not exempt even with
   implicit logging off. From 2027-01-01 (§27001) an operator may serve such a feed only if it "has reasonably
   determined that the user is not a minor". New York's final rules (announced 2026-07-28, published in the State Register 2026-07-29) take effect 180 days later, on 2027-01-25,
   with age assurance by estimation, inference or verification. A self-attested 18+ checkbox is unlikely to
   qualify in New York. Verified (statute text; New York Attorney General release). Whether the service meets the
   "addictive internet-based service" threshold is UNVERIFIED and belongs in the attorney review (idea 5); New
   York's rules reportedly reach platforms whose users spend at least 20% of their time on addictive feeds
   (UNVERIFIED against the rule text).
7. **F-3's "Why" is stale, and its Out-of-scope line contradicts "ready".** "Why" still says billing "stays

**Conflicts (decisions keeper).**
- F-3 Out of scope: "Building any of this before Phase 1's supply gate passes", against F-3 2026-11-01 "F-3 is

- F-3 criterion: a trader "is never shown a price, a plan or a card form". Connected traders can't buy Record tools.
- The price rationale cites copy-trading products, which POLICY F5 forbids the product to be.

**Verdicts.**

| Member | R1 → R2 | Key reason |
|---|---|---|
| BL | flag (timing) → flag | Compare tools are worth little at 50 traders; live billing after the gate and 30 Moderate records |
| UX | flag → flag | No visible answer to "why pay?"; Demo preview; price page says records stay free |
| LG | flag, medium → flag, medium | ROSCA and California's auto-renewal law; checkout disclosures and reminders |
| TS | flag, low-moderate → flag | Card testing and chargebacks; clear refund path; no live charge before the gates |
| GR | flag → flag | No price on any landing from an outside link; live billing after the gates |

| CS | pass with conditions → same | Billing before supply is self-defeating; Demo preview plus refund window |

**Debate points.**
- Finance argued a card-up-front trial costs little and usually converts. The decisions keeper showed that a trial
  test contradicts the owner's "no free trial at launch". It moved to the owner as a question for after launch.
- Business lead and growth added a floor of 30 Moderate-tier records before live billing, because compare tools
  would show "too early" ranges. Finance and red team agreed.
- Annual-as-default at checkout (BL R1) conflicts with COMPLIANCE §4 (no pre-checked choices). Resolved: no plan
  preselected, both prices equal.

**Verified facts.**
- ROSCA, 15 U.S.C. §8403: clear disclosure before billing details are collected, express informed consent,
  simple cancellation. Verified (statute text).
- California's auto-renewal law (Bus. & Prof. Code §§17600-17606, amended by AB 2863, Stats. 2024 ch. 515, for
  contracts from 2025-07-01): express consent, acknowledgment, online cancellation, annual notice, price-change
  notice 7 to 30 days before the change. Verified (through summaries of the bill text). §17603 (unconditional
  gift): verified.
- Annual reminder windows: Colorado 25-40 days before renewal of terms of a year or more, and before a renewal
  that extends a shorter plan past 12, 24 or 36 months (C.R.S. §6-1-732); New York's consumer auto-renewal law
  is Gen. Bus. Law §527-a (amended, reportedly in force 2025-11-05: affirmative consent, material terms, notice of
  material changes); a 15-45 day reminder window appears in pending 2025 bills (S5615, A3928-B) and is UNVERIFIED
  as enacted law (an earlier draft used 3-21 days); Virginia 30-60 days (UNVERIFIED text); Massachusetts
  UNVERIFIED. Partly true. Corrected 2026-11-01 after review: an earlier version cited N.Y. Gen. Oblig. Law §5-903,
  which governs service and maintenance contracts, not consumer subscriptions. The adopted 30-40 day annual
  reminder sits inside Colorado's 25-40 days and inside the pending New York 15-45 days, so it holds either way;
  recheck when the New York bills' status is confirmed.
- The FTC's 2024 negative-option rule was vacated (8th Cir. No. 24-3137, 2025-07-08). An advance notice of
  proposed rulemaking was published 2026-03-13; no proposed rule yet. Verified.
- $99.99 against 12 × $9.99 = $119.88 is a 16.6% saving. "About 17%" is true; "2 months free" is not. Verified.
- Card-up-front trials convert better than no trial for consumer products: UNVERIFIED (no study compares trial
  against no trial on total visitor-to-paid).

**Chair's recommendation.**
- *Recommended option:* keep $9.99 / $99.99 and no trial. Build in the vendor's test mode. `LIVE_BILLING=on` only
  after the DESIGN §3.5 gates pass and at least 30 traders have a Moderate-tier record. The owner rewords F-3's
  Out-of-scope line and "Why" to match.
- *Conditions (proposed to the owner):*
  - The checkout shows price, interval, "renews automatically until you cancel", next charge date, cancellation
    method and tax, with an unchecked consent box beside them. No plan preselected.
  - An acknowledgment email with the same terms and a cancel link.
  - Annual plans: a reminder 30-40 days before renewal. Monthly plans: California's annual notice, and a notice
    25-40 days before months 12, 24 and 36 (Colorado).
  - Online cancellation in no more steps than sign-up. A save offer at most once.
  - Savings copy says "about 17% less than 12 monthly payments", never "2 months free".
  - The trial flow in F-3's Design outlook stays unbuilt.
  - No price, Subscribe prompt or interstitial on profiles or on landings from outside links.
  - A free preview of Record tools on Demo traders (a feature preview, not a time trial).
  - The 14-day refund (12c) stands in for a trial and is never called "risk-free".
  - The copy-trading price rationale stays out of public copy.
  - The owner confirms that connected traders can't buy Record tools, or changes it. If kept, one plain line says
    so to the trader.
- *Dissent:* finance would test a trial against no trial once billing is live. That is the owner's call.
- *What would change the call:* a priced notify-me join rate that implies conversion above break-even (idea 14),
  or the owner choosing to test a trial.
- *Cheapest test:* an in-app "Notify me when it opens" control for signed-in viewers, showing the real price and
  "nothing is charged now". Count joins per 1,000 signed-in viewers.

**Common-sense check.** Pass with conditions. Charging before there is supply sells a comparison of nothing. The
conditions fix that without reopening the price or the no-trial decision.

### 12a: Disputes

**Debate points.** Finance, trust and safety and common sense first chose courts: mass-arbitration fees fall on the
company per claimant, and forced arbitration looks like a trap in a trust product. Legal answered that the exposure
that matters at this size is a class action under per-user statutes, which needs one named plaintiff and no
recruiting, while mass arbitration needs scale. A class waiver is enforceable only inside an arbitration agreement.
The fee risk is capped by design: a provider with mass-filing rules, a bellwether stage, timely payment, and a court
fallback if any part is struck. Finance and trust and safety moved to arbitration for that reason. Common sense held
courts with low confidence and asked that the clause go into the attorney review. Final count: eight for individual
arbitration with a 30-day opt-out, one for courts.

**Verified facts.** 563 U.S. 333 (2011): class waivers in arbitration agreements enforceable under the FAA (cited;
standard holding). 30 F.4th 849 (9th Cir. 2022), applied in 100 F.4th 1005 (2024) and 135 F.4th 699 (2025): notice
must be conspicuous and assent unambiguous; notice below the button failed. Verified. 2 Cal.5th 945 (2017): a
waiver of public injunctive relief is unenforceable. Verified. 120 F.4th 670 (9th Cir. 2024): one provider's
mass-arbitration rules held unconscionable; rehearing denied; later decisions enforced better-drafted protocols.
Verified. 18 Cal.5th 310 (2025): late fee payment is risky but not an automatic forfeiture. Partly true (corrects
legal). 129 F.4th 1176: UNVERIFIED, dropped. Specific mass-filing fee amounts: UNVERIFIED.

**Chair's decision.** Individual arbitration with a 30-day opt-out.
- *Conditions:* opt-out by plain email within 30 days of first assent, shown in the plain-language summary; assent by
  an unchecked box beside a visible link, above the button; small claims open to both sides; the company pays every
  arbitration fee beyond a consumer filing fee, on time, on an owner-run payment calendar; no waiver of public
  injunctive relief; a mass-filing protocol with bellwether cases in which claimants' counsel takes part, and no
  provider rules of the kind held unconscionable; an informal-resolution period of 60 days or less; severability with
  court as the fallback for any claim the waiver can't cover; re-review at 10,000 payers or when the provider's fee
  schedule changes; first item in the attorney review (idea 5).
- *Dissent:* common sense (courts with small claims at launch).
- *What would change the call:* an attorney finding the protocol likely unenforceable in the chosen venue. Then use
  courts with small claims.

- *Common-sense check:* passes with the opt-out and the plain summary, which answer the "trap" reading.

## Three highest risks in owner-decided ideas

1. **Ideas 5 and 11 remove both independent checks at once.** There is no licensed attorney and no human merge,
   the Doctor already merges protected loop files, there is no spend cap, and AI findings made without counsel are
   likely discoverable (S.D.N.Y. No. 25-cr-00503).
2. **Ideas 9 and 6 depend on data the vendor's terms restrict.** SnapTrade's developer terms bar redistributing
   market data and re-packaging end-user data to third parties without written consent, which reaches unrealized
   values, position sizes and possibly the public record itself; the vendor can cut access.
3. **F-2's ordering (ideas 7 and 8) meets the minors' feed laws in 2027.** Like-based ordering counts as an
   addictive feed in California from 2027-01-01 and New York from 2027-01-25 even with logging off; a self-attested
   18+ checkbox likely won't suffice; indefinite retention adds CCPA necessity risk.
